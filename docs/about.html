About this site.

What UmucoPulse is

UmucoPulse is a site for people, groups and stories around Rwandan culture.

You can look at public profiles, read stories after they are reviewed, and ask to work together. Phone numbers and emails stay private.

The problem

Much dance, poetry, music, craft and oral history still lives in memory, on phones, or in small circles. When it is not documented with consent and context, it is harder for younger people, educators and diaspora communities to find and continue it.

Why preservation matters

Heritage stays alive when people can practise it, teach it, and pass it on. Digital tools should support that work with respect, attribution and cultural sensitivity — not extract it.

Mission

To give practitioners, learners, educators, diaspora members and community groups a careful place to document culture and find one another.

Vision

A growing public record of living heritage that could later support education, research and cultural organisations — without claiming any official mandate today.

How the platform works

Visitors browse. Contributors register, build a cultural profile, add portfolio notes, and submit stories with mandatory consent. An administrator reviews sensitivity before anything is published to Voices. Collaboration requests never expose private phone numbers or emails.

Who it is for

People who practise culture, people who want to learn, teachers, groups, and Rwandans living abroad.

What might come later

Later versions could add curated practitioner review, location mapping and richer statistics. This prototype only stores data on this device or local server.

How UmucoPulse started

A student-led beginning

UmucoPulse began as a student project by Sylvie Umutoni Rutaganira. The founder is part of that origin story. The platform itself is for the community, not a personal portfolio.
